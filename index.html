The celestial sphere

Stars are so far away that, seen from Earth, they all seem to sit on one huge sphere centred on us. We only use directions, so only angles on this sphere matter. Earth is a point compared to it, which is why two observers at different places see a star in the same angular position.

Motion 1 · diurnal

The whole sphere (stars, sun, planets) turns east→west once per sidereal day about the celestial axis PP′, the extension of Earth's rotation axis. Drag the Hours slider: relative positions never change.

Motion 2 · intrinsic

Superimposed on this, the sun, moon and planets slowly drift west→east against the stars. The sun traces one fixed great circle in a year, the ecliptic, tilted by ε = 23°26′ to the celestial equator. Move Sun λ☉.

The vernal equinox V

The two circles cross at two points. V is the one where the sun crosses the equator going north called the vernal equinox or spring equinox; the other is the autumnal equinox V′.

V is not stationary with respect to the fixed stars. Search precession of equinoxes to learn more

How the system is built

We use three mutually perpendicular unit vectors to define celestial coordinates: v points to the vernal equinox, p to the north celestial pole, and u completes the set (on the equator, 90° east of V). Any direction can be written r = xv + yu + zp, and each number is the dot product with the axis: x = r·v, etc.

Equatorial plane: the plane containing the celestial equator (Earth's equator extended).
Declination δ: angle of R above or below the equatorial plane (±90° at the poles).
Right ascension α: project R onto the equatorial plane, then measure the angle from V eastward, 0° - 360° (or 0 - 24 h, 15° = 1 h).

■ ecliptic (fundamental circle)   ■ celestial equator for comparison

How the system is built

Ecliptic the ecliptic is the sun's apparent path across the celestial sphere

Ecliptic plane: the plane containing the ecliptic. It is tilted ε = 23°26′ because Earth's axis is tilted. Its poles are Q (north) and Q′.

Origin: V lies on both circles, so it is the zero of λ too.

Ecliptic longitude λ runs along the ecliptic, west→east, in the sun's direction of travel.

Ecliptic latitude β is the angle above/below the ecliptic plane.

The planets stay within a few degrees of β = 0, which is why this system suits the sun, moon and planets.

New axes: v points to the vernal equinox, s to the summer solstice, at λ = 90° and q to the ecliptic pole.

■ local horizon (fundamental circle)   ■ celestial equator

How the system is built

This one belongs to an observer on the surface of the earth at site X.

Horizon: the plane tangent to the earth at X, with zenith Z overhead. and nadir Z' underfoot.

Local meridian: the plane plane that passes through the north and south compass point and the zenith. The north celestial pole lies on the meridian plane.

Latitude L: the angle between the north compass point and the north celestial pole. The horizon therefore tilted by 90° - L with respect to the equator.

Altitude a: angle above the horizon.

Azimuth A: angle from the north point, toward east (N 0°, E 90°, S 180°, W 270°).

Axes: n (north), e (east), z (zenith).

Because of diurnal motion, α₀, the right ascension currently on the meridian, grows 15° per hour. Drag it and the horizon turns under the stars: a and A of a fixed star change continuously.
